# Business Insights
## Executive Summary
The analysis shows that revenue continued to grow during the study period, but the quality of growth changed significantly. In 2025, revenue growth was mainly supported by improved pricing or a higher-value product mix. In H1 2026, sales volume increased faster than revenue, indicating potential pricing pressure or a shift toward lower-priced products.
1. Revenue growth slowed in 2026
- Net revenue reached approximately VND 49.22 trillion in 2025, an increase of 4.8% compared with 2024.
- Sales quantity increased by only 1.1%, while revenue per unit rose by approximately 3.7%.
- This indicates that 2025 growth came primarily from pricing improvements or a shift toward higher-value products rather than volume expansion.
- In H1 2026, revenue grew by only 1.2% YoY, despite quantity increasing by 5.2%.
Business implication: The company continued to sell more units in 2026, but each unit generated less revenue. Management should investigate discounting, contract pricing and changes in product mix.
2. Revenue per unit declined in H1 2026
- Estimated revenue per unit decreased by approximately 3.8% YoY in H1 2026.
- This suggests that volume growth did not translate proportionally into revenue growth.
- Possible explanations include heavier discounting, stronger sales of lower-priced products or increased competitive pressure.
Recommended action: Monitor revenue per unit by product group, principal and channel. Products with high volume growth but declining revenue per unit should be reviewed for margin erosion.
3. Channel performance became increasingly uneven
Compared with H1 2025:
- Trade increased by approximately 68.7%.
- Animal Health increased by approximately 25.9%.
- Hospital declined by approximately 13.5%.
- Retail declined by approximately 2.4%.
Business implication: Growth is shifting away from Hospital and Retail toward Trade and Animal Health. This creates growth opportunities but may also increase dependence on a smaller number of channels.
Recommended action: Identify the customers and product groups driving Trade growth, while reviewing lost accounts, tender performance and product availability in the Hospital channel.
4. Sales displayed a recurring monthly pattern
Revenue in 2025 fluctuated between approximately VND 3.4 trillion and VND 4.9 trillion per month, with stronger performance around March, June, August and December.
Business implication: The repeated peaks may reflect purchasing cycles, tender schedules, promotional activity or inventory replenishment.
Recommended action: Align inventory planning and sales campaigns with the months that historically generate stronger demand. The cause of the pattern should be validated before treating it as confirmed seasonality.
5. Product performance is concentrated
A limited number of product groups generate a relatively large proportion of mapped product revenue, while many products contribute considerably smaller amounts.
Business implication: High concentration makes revenue sensitive to the performance, supply availability and pricing of a small number of products or principals.
## Recommended action:
- Protect availability and supplier relationships for core products.
- Review low-performing products for rationalisation.
- Identify products with growing volume but weakening revenue per unit.
- Avoid relying only on total revenue; include contribution margin when cost data becomes available.
6. Customer performance requires retention monitoring
The customer analysis shows a small group of customers contributing substantially more revenue than the remaining customer base.
Business implication: Losing one major customer could materially affect performance, while the large long-tail customer base may contain cross-selling opportunities.
Recommended action: Introduce customer segmentation based on revenue, growth, purchase frequency and recency. Key accounts should receive separate retention and development plans.
7. Data-quality issues can affect business conclusions
The dataset contains several limitations:
- 29,094 records have missing quantity or revenue information.
- Negative revenue values may represent returns, credit notes or data errors.
- Customer names contain inconsistent spacing and encoding.
- Placeholder customer values such as ZZZCCC are present.
- The 2026 dataset contains only partial-year data, including an incomplete July period.
Business implication: These issues can distort customer counts, channel attribution, product totals and YoY comparisons.
Recommended action: Add a data-quality layer that standardises customer and product keys, separates returns from normal sales, and excludes incomplete periods from like-for-like comparisons.
## Recommendations
Based on the analysis, the business should prioritise:
1. Investigating the decline in revenue per unit during 2026.
2. Protecting high-growth Trade and Animal Health accounts.
3. Developing a recovery plan for the Hospital channel.
4. Monitoring revenue concentration by customer and product.
5. Separating returns and credit notes from gross sales.
6. Comparing 2026 using H1-versus-H1 figures instead of full-year comparisons.
7. Improving master-data mapping before using Channel and ATC results for management decisions.
# Portfolio conclusion
This project demonstrates how transactional sales data can be transformed into decision-oriented insights. The analysis identified a shift from price/mix-led growth in 2025 to volume-led but lower-value growth in H1 2026, significant differences in channel performance, and material data-quality risks affecting customer and product reporting.
